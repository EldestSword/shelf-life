# Shelf Life: Lou’s Library

## Play

Twenty cells, five columns and four rows. Tap one book then another of the exact same level to merge. Six tiers run from Single volume to Complete archive. A top-tier archive does not merge again. Tap an empty cell to move; the swap control exchanges unlike items. Dragging is also supported. Return any unwanted item for free to release a cell; higher-tier books and completed dishes ask for confirmation. Undo restores the last board change.

Donations are free and immediate. A pantry delivery requires two free cells and supplies the named cheese and its prepared ingredients. Combining those exact components makes the named dish. The ingredient tile includes cooking and seasoning; this is not a cooking simulator or a substitute for the referenced recipe.

Three active requests are always present, paged with arrows. Fulfilment removes the exact book tier and optional exact dish, gives funds and replaces that request. Eight recurring visitors have four dialogue stages, advancing after every three completed requests from that person. Requests have no deadline.

## Growth

Start with 60 funds. A book request pays `10 × 2^(tier−1)` plus the recipe value, if applicable. Upgrades add stated donation probabilities, percentage bonuses and flat rewards. The dresser modifies the dish portion before room multipliers; the lamps and atlas add flat amounts afterwards. Percentages are additive. All costs and effects appear before purchase.

Twelve improvements visibly change the library: trolley, nook, lamps, dresser, plants, cabinet, club table, atlas shelves, terrace, stained glass, archive loft and festival. The plants unlock three palettes. The festival requires all eleven earlier improvements. Play continues afterwards.

Three visitors in a local calendar day unlock 75 funds, claimable once. Skipping any number of days loses nothing. Eight milestones give one-time bonuses. Discovering each of the ten cheese preparations records it in the Cheese Atlas.

## Word Vault

Two distinct seven-letter target words; nine guesses shared across both. Every accepted guess receives independent feedback for both words. Correct positions are counted first; misplaced matches consume only the remaining copies of that letter. A solved grid freezes, while the other continues. Choose a grid header to view that word’s keyboard evidence. The contrast setting adds symbols to coloured feedback.

There are 49 curated difficult targets and 22,298 accepted guesses. The dictionary is deliberately permissive and includes names and regional variants; the target pool is editorially restricted. Definitions and usage notes appear after every finished puzzle, including losses. No answer clues are exposed during play.

Solve both for 140 funds; solve one by the ninth guess for 30; solve neither for zero. There is no loss of existing funds. Daily puzzles use the local date; practice pairs use a separate seeded sequence and can be replaced without penalty. Rewards are recorded once per completed puzzle.

## Presentation

The recovered screenshots govern composition, palette, typography and navigation. Lou’s coarse original sprite was explicitly rejected: the completed art instead uses a 64 × 80 figure with shaded brown hair, a loose bun, tortoiseshell frames and four expressive faces. Room furnishing is present from the start and becomes denser with purchases. All art ships locally.

Motion uses short tile responses, pixel-step idle/celebration movement and restrained particles. Both the in-game setting and the operating system’s reduced-motion preference are respected. Sound is synthesised locally and off by default.
