# Shelf Life: Lou’s Library

## Play

Twenty cells, five columns and four rows. Books belong to Fiction, Ideas & essays, Poetry, History, Natural world or Art & music. Tap one book then another with the exact same section and level to merge. Six tiers run from Single volume to Complete archive. A top-tier archive does not merge again. Acquisitions sells cheaper random arrivals from open collections or a dearer exact subject order. Fiction and Ideas & essays start open; Poetry, History, Natural world and Art & music require visitor progress and library funds. Tap an empty cell to move; the swap control exchanges unlike items. Dragging is also supported. Return any unwanted item for free to release a cell; higher-tier books and completed dishes ask for confirmation. Undo restores the last board change.

Arrivals are immediate but cost library funds: six for a random arrival or ten for an exact subject order. A pantry stock order also costs funds, needs between two and seven free cells and supplies every named component from the cited preparation. Later recipe cards must be researched once before their stock can be ordered. Two different components from the same recipe form a partial preparation; continue adding any missing component until the dish is complete. Components from another recipe and duplicate components are rejected. This is not a cooking simulator or a substitute for the referenced recipe.

Three active requests are always present, paged with arrows. Fulfilment removes the exact book section and tier plus the optional exact dish, gives funds and replaces that request. Twelve recurring visitors have subject preferences and four dialogue stages, advancing after every three completed requests from that person. Requests have no deadline.

The first-run guided opening uses the real starter board. It asks the player to merge two exact Fiction books, assemble Manchego with membrillo, serve the resulting request and open the Journal. It can be skipped, and the four-page help remains available afterwards.

## Growth

Start with 60 funds. A book request pays `10 × 2^(tier−1)` plus the recipe value, if applicable. Upgrades add stated arrival probabilities, percentage bonuses and flat rewards. The dresser modifies the dish portion before room multipliers; the lamps and atlas add flat amounts afterwards. Percentages are additive. All costs and effects appear before purchase.

Sixteen improvements visibly change the library: trolley, nook, lamps, dresser, conservatory, cabinet, club table, listening alcove, atlas shelves, card catalogue, terrace, bindery, stained glass, periodicals gallery, archive loft and festival. The conservatory unlocks three teal and sea-green palettes. The festival requires all fifteen earlier improvements. Play continues afterwards.

Three visitors in a local calendar day unlock 75 funds, claimable once. Skipping any number of days loses nothing. Eight milestones give one-time bonuses. Discovering each of the 24 cheese preparations records it in the Cheese Atlas. The Book Catalogue separately records all 36 section-and-level combinations.

## Word Vault

One seven-letter target word with six guesses. Every accepted guess receives correct-position and misplaced-letter feedback. Correct positions are counted first; misplaced matches consume only the remaining copies of that letter. The contrast setting adds symbols to coloured feedback.

There are 93 defined targets retained for save compatibility and 22,300 accepted guesses. New puzzles use a 40-word familiar core pool four days out of five and a 30-word moderate challenge pool otherwise. Extremely specialist legacy terms such as PLEROMA remain defined so old saves validate but are never selected for a new puzzle. Definitions and usage or etymology notes appear after every finished puzzle, including losses.

A solve earns 80 funds; a loss earns zero and removes nothing. Daily puzzles use the local date; practice words use a separate seeded sequence and can be replaced without penalty. Rewards are recorded once per completed puzzle.

## Presentation

The recovered screenshots govern composition, palette, typography and navigation. Lou’s coarse original sprite was explicitly rejected. The current art uses a smooth 160 × 200 cel-shaded vector figure with a rounded silhouette, layered brown hair, loose bun, tortoiseshell frames, striped top, teal overshirt and four expressive faces. It resembles a modern illustrated game character rather than a photograph or early-console sprite. Room furnishing is present from the start and becomes denser with purchases. All art ships locally.

Motion uses short tile responses, eased idle and celebration movement and restrained particles. Both the in-game setting and the operating system’s reduced-motion preference are respected. Sound is synthesised locally and off by default.
