# Editorial and art rules

Write for an intelligent British adult. Prefer concrete information and dry wit to encouragement. Swearing is occasional: visitor story progression, an infrequent service response, or every third successful vault. Never aim abuse at Lou or the player.

No decorative drink vessels, slogans, motivational messages, fake quote boards or sentimental wall art. Keep screen copy concise. The automated checker covers known banned phrases, but visual review and editorial judgement remain necessary.

Cheese entries need stable IDs, actual cheese names, specified preparations, a region or tradition, concise ingredients, an original factual description and provenance links. Choose a documented variation, rather than imply that one recipe exhausts a living regional tradition. Omit uncertain claims. The current entry for Imeretian khachapuri is deliberately distinct from Adjarian khachapuri.

Targets must be seven ASCII letters, legitimate English headwords and confidently definable. Most new puzzles should use familiar educated vocabulary; the smaller challenge pool may be sharper without becoming specialist trivia. Avoid arbitrary proper nouns, abbreviations and padding with inflections. Definitions and usage notes must be authored, not copied from a dictionary. The broad guess dictionary does not determine answer selection.

Characters and objects use crisp, locally rendered SVG with restrained shading, clean silhouettes and enough detail to remain readable at phone size. Lou’s full figure uses a 160 × 200 viewBox and four expressive poses. Her face needs visible tortoiseshell glasses, brown gathered hair, a readable expression and a rounded silhouette. She should look like a modern illustrated game character, neither a coarse early-console sprite nor a photoreal rendering. Visitors, books, cheeses and room objects share the same polished visual family. Original photographs and handover references must remain outside the runtime and repository.
